# GOAL CONSTITUTION: Project Cutaway Reel

## 1. Strategy and Vision

Welcome to Project Cutaway. Our overarching objective is to produce a narrated, animated comedy video strictly under two minutes in length, explicitly optimized and formatted for Instagram Reels (9:16 aspect ratio). The humor must deeply resonate with the comedic sensibilities of *South Park* and *Family Guy*—meaning it should be highly satirical, unafraid of taboo or edgy subjects, heavily reliant on absurd escalation, and utilize structural comedic devices like the cutaway gag. However, under no circumstances will we copy or use any existing characters, IP, or specific locations from those shows. We are synthesizing their *rhythm* and *tone*, not their exact copyrighted content.

Crucially, our swarm operates under strict hardware and API capabilities: we currently possess zero text-to-video, zero text-to-speech, and zero image generation capabilities. Therefore, what "excellent" looks like for this specific project is a brilliant pivot to code-as-medium. We will deliver a self-playing, standalone HTML/CSS/JavaScript bundle. The animation will be achieved via CSS keyframes and DOM-manipulated SVG graphics, mimicking the crude but expressive construction-paper aesthetic of early *South Park*. The narration will be handled natively by the browser's Web Speech API (`window.speechSynthesis`), manipulating pitch and rate to create distinct character voices. The end user will simply open the HTML file, click "Play" (to bypass browser autoplay restrictions), and screen-record the result on their mobile device or desktop to post directly to Instagram.

The two decisions that matter most for this project are:

1. **Comedic Timing and Pacing:** In comedy, timing is everything. Because our visual aesthetic will be intentionally crude and code-driven, the script's sharpness and the JavaScript-controlled timing of punchlines, awkward pauses, and sudden cutaways must be flawless. The animation loop acts as our comedic director.
2. **Platform Constraints:** An Instagram Reel demands immediate attention. The animation must start with a hard narrative hook within the first 3 seconds, utilize large, highly readable kinetic typography (subtitles) synchronized with the Web Speech API (as many IG users watch on mute initially), and strictly adhere to the 9:16 vertical viewport frame.

## 2. Style Decisions and Conventions

To ensure seamless handoffs between the writing, directing, and coding agents within the swarm, the following conventions are immutable laws.

**Format, Look, and File Constraints:**
Deliverables must be plain text code files intended to run in a local browser: `index.html`, `styles.css`, and `app.js`. No external assets (image files, custom external fonts, or MP3s) may be linked or requested. All visual assets must be inline SVGs generated by the coding agents. The CSS must enforce a strict `aspect-ratio: 9/16` container, centered on the screen with a `max-width` of 1080px and `max-height` of 1920px, mimicking a mobile viewport perfectly. The visual style should be defined as "crude vector art"—bold, flat colors, simple geometric shapes, and exaggerated, snappy movements. This turns our lack of image-generation models into a deliberate, charming stylistic choice.

**Sound, Narration, and Pacing:**
Narration is exclusively driven by the browser's `window.speechSynthesis`. The script and accompanying JavaScript must dynamically adjust `pitch` and `rate` parameters to differentiate at least three distinct characters (e.g., a high-pitched frantic protagonist, a deep deadpan antagonist, and an over-enunciating announcer). Because raw TTS can feel slightly robotic, the script should lean into deadpan and awkward humor, where the robotic delivery enhances the absurdity of the dialogue. The pacing must be relentless. Ensure a minimum of two visual or contextual cutaway gags (e.g., "This is worse than the time I...") to honor the targeted comedic style.

**The Integration Contract:**
Every agent acting as a producer must ship a `manifest.md` file alongside its deliverable. Every consumer agent must validate the input against this manifest before beginning its execution step.

The `manifest.md` must explicitly define:
- **Asset Manifestation:** A list of all SVG IDs, DOM elements, and their intended visual representations (e.g., `svg-character-bob`, `svg-bg-kitchen`).
- **Timeline Cues:** A precise chronological map in milliseconds dictating the JavaScript `setTimeout` or `requestAnimationFrame` choreography (e.g., `0ms: Scene 1 init`, `2500ms: Bob speaks line 1`, `4200ms: Cutaway gag triggers`).
- **Voice Profiles:** The exact string identifiers, `pitch` (0.0 to 2.0), and `rate` (0.1 to 10.0) float values for every speaking character to guarantee continuity across scenes.

## 3. Scoring Rubric

Every deliverable will be judged across three dimensions. The numeric pass threshold is 8.0/10, with a strict margin of 0.5 triggering automatic revision loops for any score below an 8.0.

**Dimension 1: Comedic Impact and Writing (Weight: 40%)**
This measures the script's adherence to the satirical, absurd tone, and its structural execution.
- **Score 4:** The humor is safe, cliché, or relies on tired internet memes. The narrative is highly linear with no subversion of expectations. It feels like a generic, sanitized corporate video attempting to be "fun" rather than an edgy late-night comedy.
- **Score 7:** The writing captures the edgy tone and successfully executes a cutaway gag or an absurd escalation. However, the dialogue may be slightly bloated, or the punchlines are highly predictable. It is funny, but feels highly derivative rather than an original take on the genre.
- **Score 9:** Brilliant, sharp satire. The script introduces a mundane premise and rapidly escalates it into complete absurdity. The cutaway gags are jarring, logically disconnected but structurally hilarious. The dialogue is extremely tight, leaving no dead air, and cleverly uses the robotic nature of the Web Speech API as a meta-joke. (Exemplar references: *South Park*'s "World of Warcraft" deadpan pacing or *Family Guy*'s famously long, awkward pauses used purely to build uncomfortable comedic tension).

**Dimension 2: Technical Animation and SVG Execution (Weight: 30%)**
This measures the swarm's use of HTML/CSS/JS to create a compelling visual experience entirely from scratch without external generative media assets.
- **Score 4:** The screen is mostly static. SVGs are broken, malformed, or misaligned. The animation consists only of basic opacity fading. It completely fails to utilize the vertical 9:16 frame effectively, leaving massive amounts of dead space.
- **Score 7:** Characters and backgrounds are visually distinct and charming. CSS keyframes are used for basic but effective walking or talking animations (e.g., scaling or bouncing DOM elements to simulate speaking). The 9:16 layout is populated properly.
- **Score 9:** Masterful use of DOM manipulation. Characters possess expressive (albeit geometrically simple) reactions. Transitions between the main narrative and the cutaway gags are instantaneous, visually distinct, and jarringly effective. Kinetic typography (subtitles) animate dynamically on screen, keeping the viewer's eye moving constantly.

**Dimension 3: Platform Fit and Audio Sync (Weight: 30%)**
This measures the timing choreography, the TTS implementation stability, and the ultimate suitability for an Instagram Reel.
- **Score 4:** The Web Speech API triggers randomly, overlapping voices into a chaotic mess. Subtitles are missing, sized poorly, or clip off the edge of the mobile frame. The total runtime exceeds 120 seconds or fails to hook the viewer in the critical first 5 seconds.
- **Score 7:** Voices are distinct and properly sequenced. Subtitles are present, readable, and generally match the audio. The timeline generally aligns with the script, and the video strictly respects the under-2-minute rule.
- **Score 9:** Absolute programmatic precision. The JavaScript event loop perfectly syncs visual actions (like a character's mouth moving or jumping) with the `speechSynthesis` API's `onboundary` events. Subtitles pop word-by-word with perfect timing. The video demands attention in the first 2 seconds with a loud, absurd visual or auditory hook tailor-made to stop scrolling IG users in their tracks.

## 4. Judge Instructions

When evaluating a deliverable, the judging agent (utilizing the `gemini-3.1-pro-preview` model for hard planning and reasoning) must adopt the persona of a ruthlessly objective comedy showrunner and senior technical lead.

1. **Blind Evaluation:** Score the raw output (whether it is the raw script, the compiled HTML code, or the intermediate manifest) strictly on its own merits. Do not factor in the previous iterations, the perceived effort, or the specific sub-agent that produced it.
2. **Side-by-Side Anchoring:** You must keep the rubric dimensions explicitly open in your context window. Explicitly compare the deliverable against the specific 4, 7, and 9 anchors before generating a final number.
3. **Baseline Comparison:** Always compare the deliverable against the "laziest acceptable version" (which for this project is a static colored screen with sequential TTS and plain, unstyled text subtitles). If the deliverable does not significantly elevate itself above this baseline through deliberate comedic timing, snappy SVG visual charm, or dynamic typography, it cannot score above a 5.0.
4. **Justification Format:** You must cite the nearest rubric anchor and provide exactly one line of concrete justification mapping the failure or success to the code/script. 
   - *Format Example:* "Score: 7.2. Nearest Anchor: 7 - The script includes a solid cutaway gag, but the justification is that lines 45-50 are overly verbose and ruin the fast pacing required for a successful Instagram Reel."

Any score generated below an 8.0 must automatically trigger a mandatory revision loop. The judge must output a strict, bulleted list detailing exactly which functions, CSS classes, or specific lines of dialogue require adjustment to meet the passing threshold.
