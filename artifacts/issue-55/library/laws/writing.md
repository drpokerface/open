# writing and documents - scripts, rubrics, plans, reports (pack: writing)
- THE CLIMB: draft the COMPLETE document early (rough, every section present), then
  improve it in judged loops - a fresh judge scores the whole against the rubric, names
  the single weakest dimension, you make ONE targeted improvement, the judge looks again.
  Never polish a section while the whole does not exist; never call a one-shot draft
  finished - the last passes of the climb are where excellence lives.
- Anchored, specific, measurable: every rubric descriptor, style rule, or convention names
  something a reader could check - a count, a length, an example, what the first line
  does. Vague adjectives ("engaging", "high quality") are placeholders in disguise.
- Contracts: a document consumed by another task ships manifest.md beside it - exact
  filenames, formats, one tiny sample - and a consumer validates against it FIRST.
- Degenerate twins for documents: blank, truncated, a section missing, adjectives with
  no anchors, the spec text copy-pasted back as content, placeholders. Write them as
  twins/ directories and make verify.py reject each with a specific mechanical check
  (section present, anchor count, length floor, placeholder scan) plus the judge.
- Scripts and narration: read them aloud in your head for timing; count words per second
  against the medium's norm; a 2-minute video is ~260-300 spoken words.
